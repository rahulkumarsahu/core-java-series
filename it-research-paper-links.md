# IT and Computer Science Research Paper Directory

**Last verified:** 24 August 2026  
**Coverage:** DevOps, CI/CD, software engineering, AI/ML, backend engineering, distributed systems, system design, databases, networking, cloud infrastructure, security, programming languages, HCI, and algorithms.

No single website contains every IT paper or provides every paper free of charge. Use the broad indexes to discover papers, then use the open-access repositories and official proceedings to obtain the full text legally.

## Legend

- **Open:** Most or all full text is free.
- **Index:** Excellent for discovery and citations; full-text availability varies.
- **Proceedings:** Peer-reviewed conference or journal publications.
- **Practice:** Industry research, operational reports, or engineering references rather than conventional academic papers.
- **Paywall possible:** Some abstracts are free but full papers may require institutional access or payment.

## Finding a Legal Free Copy of a Paywalled Paper

1. Search the exact title in [Google Scholar](https://scholar.google.com/) and check the right-hand PDF links.
2. Search the title or DOI in [Semantic Scholar](https://www.semanticscholar.org/), [CORE](https://core.ac.uk/), or [OpenAlex](https://explore.openalex.org/works/).
3. Check [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/) for an author preprint.
4. Check the author's university or research-lab publication page.
5. Use the [Unpaywall browser extension](https://unpaywall.org/products/extension) to locate a legal open-access copy.
6. If none exists, use a university/public library or contact the author politely.

## Recommended Weekly Reading Workflow

1. Pick one narrow topic, such as `deployment rollback`, `distributed tracing`, `RAG evaluation`, or `database replication`.
2. Find one recent survey or systematic literature review through Semantic Scholar or Google Scholar.
3. Use its references to identify three foundational papers.
4. Use “cited by” to identify three recent follow-up papers.
5. Confirm the publication venue and year through DBLP.
6. Read the abstract, problem, method, evaluation, threats to validity, and conclusion before reading every detail.
7. Save the paper and notes in [Zotero](https://www.zotero.org/).
8. Write five lines: problem, key idea, evidence, limitation, and how it changes an engineering decision.
